Biomarkers in Patients With Acute Ischaemic Stroke Undergoing Thrombectomy Compared With Controls: A Pilot Study.

✓ Verified summary

  1. Researchers compared three blood markers of brain damage in 21 stroke patients undergoing clot-removal surgery against 19 control patients having non-brain surgery. [1]
  2. Blood samples were collected before the procedure and at 2 and 24 hours after, then tested with a specific laboratory assay. [2]
  3. While the other two markers, NfL and tau, went up significantly in both groups, the GFAP level did not show a significant rise in the control group over the 24-hour period. [3]
  4. Because GFAP did not rise in the control group, it may be less affected by the effects of anesthesia and surgery than the other markers. [4]
  5. The results suggest that GFAP should be studied further as a possible tool for detecting acute brain injury in hospital settings. [5]
