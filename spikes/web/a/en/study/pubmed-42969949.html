The Journal of cell biology · 2 Nov 2026

ALMS1 stabilizes the proximal centriole to drive transition zone assembly and ciliogenesis.

✓ Verified summary

  1. Alström syndrome is a human ciliopathy caused by mutations in the ALMS1 gene. [1]
  2. The ALMS1 protein anchors at the base of centrioles, and its absence causes these structures to shorten and snap. [2]
  3. Broken centrioles exhibit weaker microtubule organization, while specific Golgi proteins fail to reach the centrosome. [3]
  4. ALMS1 is essential for building the transition zone, forming cilia, and regulating the ciliary membrane. [4]
  5. In mice, ALMS1 removal leads to age-related cilia loss in the retina, kidney, and brain, supporting a model where centriole damage and faulty transport hinder cilia upkeep. [5]
