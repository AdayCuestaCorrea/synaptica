The Journal of general physiology · 5 Nov 2026

Fluorescent glutamate reporter mouse line reveals slow stochastic release from rod photoreceptor cells.

✓ Verified summary

  1. Scientists examined how rod cells in mice release glutamate, a chemical messenger, by engineering the animals to express a glowing marker that reacts to this substance. [1]
  2. By tracking these chemical signals in both dim and bright lighting, the team observed that glutamate release responded more strongly to slow changes in light intensity than to rapid fluctuations. [2]
  3. The cells emitted glutamate in a random pattern at a speed of 20 to 40 packets per second per ribbon, a rate that was confirmed by a separate electrical measurement method showing about 38 packets per second. [3]
  4. Maintaining this steady release rate across different light levels helps pass visual information accurately to the next stage of the visual system. [4]
