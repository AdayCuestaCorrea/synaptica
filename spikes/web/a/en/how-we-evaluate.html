Where the news comes from

Every day a program gathers what is new from public sources: PubMed, Europe PMC, bioRxiv and medRxiv preprints, the Nature, Science and Cell RSS feeds (title, authors, date and link only) and headlines from SINC, The Conversation and ScienceDaily. Nobody hand-picks what goes into «Latest» or «Nature · Science · Cell». «Classics» are the exception: the site proposes them and only those Sofía approves are published.

The three signals of every study

Always visible, on the card and on the study page. When a piece of data does not exist the signal says «no data»: we never guess.

Review
Preprint: it comes from bioRxiv or medRxiv and has not been peer reviewed yet. Published in a journal: it appears in a journal. Note that this alone does not prove it was peer reviewed. Gap Sofía decides the final name of this signal.
Subjects
Humans, animals or both, from the MeSH descriptors added by the US National Library of Medicine. Newly published studies do not have them yet, and then the signal says «no data».
Notices
Retracted, corrected or expression of concern, from the notices publishers deposit in Crossref. We check that list every day, so a retraction that arrives years later shows up too.

How summaries are written

  1. Short facts are taken from the article's abstract, and each fact must be in the original.
  2. A language model writes the English text from those facts only, then the Spanish text.
  3. Automatic checks: every sentence cites its source; figures and names match it; never 8 or more words in a row copied; no overclaiming («cure», «proves that», «breakthrough»…); the study's caveats are kept; and the Spanish spelling is correct.
  4. A judge, a model from another family, rereads each summary. Without its approval nothing is published.
  5. If anything fails, the summary is not published: a fallback text built only from the data is shown instead (for example: «Study in animals published in Neuron on 5 November 2026»).

We never publish abstracts or paragraphs of the articles: only our own summaries, with a link to the original and its licence.

What we do not do

SYNAPTICA is science communication. It gives no medical advice and recommends no treatments.
