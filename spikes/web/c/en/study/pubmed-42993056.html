Developmental elimination of electrical synapses by UNC-51/UNC-76-mediated vesicular transport.

The Journal of cell biology · 6 Nov 2026

  • Review Published in a journal provisional
  • Subjects Animals
  • Notices No notices

Gap Provisional wording: «published in a journal» does not prove peer review. Sofía decides what this signal is called.

✓ Verified summary

  1. Researchers studying sensory neurons in the roundworm C. elegans found that electrical synapses, which are direct physical connections between cells, are temporarily present in early life before being removed as the nervous system matures. [1]
  2. These early electrical connections cause rapid fluctuations in calcium levels, a signaling molecule within the cells, and using light to destroy them stops these fluctuations while preventing the formation of standard chemical synapses. [2]
  3. However, restoring the calcium activity without the electrical connections fixes the issue with chemical synapse formation, indicating that the calcium signal itself is crucial for proper development. [3]
  4. The removal process relies on a protein called UNC-51/ULK, which modifies another protein to trigger the transport of channel proteins away from the connection site. [4]
  5. When this removal mechanism is blocked, the electrical connections persist, causing the neurons to become overly active. [5]
