Outdoor Air Pollution Exposure Alters Fear Regulation and Fear-Related Neural Circuitry in Adolescence.

Biological psychiatry global open science · 8 Nov 2026

  • Review Published in a journal provisional
  • Subjects No data
  • Notices No notices

Gap Provisional wording: «published in a journal» does not prove peer review. Sofía decides what this signal is called.

✓ Verified summary

  1. Researchers examined 102 teenagers aged 10 to 17, dividing them into two groups based on recent exposure to fine air particles relative to a 9 micrograms per cubic meter limit. [1]
  2. By measuring skin sweat, observing avoidance behaviors, and asking about fear levels, the team found that those with higher pollution exposure displayed stronger physical and emotional reactions when learning to associate a signal with a threat. [2]
  3. Participants in the high-exposure group also reported a more intense return of fear when the threat signal reappeared after the danger had passed. [3]
  4. Brain scans indicated that this group exhibited increased activity in the right amygdala and left insula, regions responsible for processing fear and safety. [4]
  5. These results suggest that altered fear processing may be one mechanism through which air pollution contributes to anxiety in young people. [5]
