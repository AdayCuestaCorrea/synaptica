Sustained delivery of 3D-bioprinted stem cell-derived small extracellular vesicles promotes neural regeneration following traumatic brain injury via the CREB1/PGC-1α/FNDC5 axis.

Bioactive materials · 8 Nov 2026

  • Review Published in a journal provisional
  • Subjects No data
  • Notices No notices

Gap Provisional wording: «published in a journal» does not prove peer review. Sofía decides what this signal is called.

✓ Verified summary

  1. Researchers developed a gelatin-based hydrogel system to retain vesicles from fat-derived stem cells at brain injury sites, addressing the rapid clearance of standard two-dimensional culture products. [1]
  2. By harvesting vesicles from stem cells grown via three-dimensional bioprinting, the team generated approximately eight times more vesicles with higher protein content than traditional flat cultures. [2]
  3. In living subjects, this delivery method remained at the injury site longer, reduced tissue damage, and improved long-term movement control and memory. [3]
  4. The treatment increased the number of dividing cells developing into nerve cells and boosted the activity of proteins like CREB1, PGC-1α, and FNDC5 in neural stem cells. [4]
  5. The authors describe this gel-based approach as a promising cell-free strategy for repairing brain tissue after injury. [5]
