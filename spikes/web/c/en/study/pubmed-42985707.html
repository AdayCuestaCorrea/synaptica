Improved Visualization of the Internal Branch of the Superior Laryngeal Nerve during Pharyngeal Endoscopic Subepithelial Dissection with Use of Red Dichromatic Imaging.

✓ Verified summary

  1. Researchers examined a 57-year-old woman who underwent surgery to remove a small, 5-millimeter surface growth in her throat. [1]
  2. In this single case, red dichromatic imaging, a technique that uses specific wavelengths to enhance contrast, made a nearby nerve appear sharper and more distinct than standard white light. [2]
  3. The tumor was removed in one piece without visible nerve damage, and the patient did not experience swallowing difficulties, voice changes, or other complications. [3]
  4. These findings suggest that the imaging method could be a helpful tool for visualizing this nerve and supporting techniques that aim to preserve it. [4]
