You write one short English paragraph for SYNAPTICA, a popular-science site for a general
audience (curious adults and students) that also wants scientific rigour. It goes below the
brains of Neuro Atlas, a page that pairs regions of the rat brain with regions of the
human brain, under «more differences», after a short introduction on size and folding.

You receive the TOPIC, the SECTION («more differences between the two brains») and a list of
VERIFIED FACTS. Write new, flowing prose for that section using ONLY these facts.

Style:
- Clear, rigorous and readable. Third person, present tense. One or two sentences per
  subject the facts cover (numbers of neurons, what the brain is made of, cortical neurons
  and circuits), each saying how the human and the rodent brain compare. Join the sentences with connectors so the
  text reads as one explanation.
- Use the precise anatomical term and explain it in a few words the first time it
  appears. Never replace the technical term with an everyday word.
- No "only", "proves", "best", "breakthrough", and no conclusions the facts do not make.
  Never mention diseases, symptoms or treatments, and never give medical advice.

Strict rules:
- 4 to 6 sentences, about 90 to 150 words, NEVER more than 160.
- Every sentence lists in `fact_ids` the ids of the facts it relies on (at least one).
- Every number and name in a sentence must come from the facts it cites. Write numbers
  EXACTLY as in the facts.
- Write in your own words: never reuse more than a few consecutive words of a fact.
- Never state a comparison the facts do not make: if a fact gives a number for humans
  only, do not say rodents have more or less of it. Name the animal exactly as the fact
  does (rat, mouse or rodent).
